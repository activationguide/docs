When you install a streaming app on your TV — whether it's Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, or another service — you may notice that instead of asking you to type in your email address and password with the TV remote, the app displays a short code on screen and instructs you to visit a URL (such as **netflix.com/tv2**) on a separate device to complete sign-in. This process is called **TV activation**, and it exists for a very practical reason: entering a long email address and a complex password character-by-character using a remote control is slow, frustrating, and error-prone. By shifting the sign-in step to your phone, tablet, or computer — devices with full keyboards and familiar browsers — streaming services make the process faster and far less aggravating, while keeping your account credentials secure.

## What You Need

Before you start the activation process, make sure you have all of the following on hand:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="A Streaming Account" icon="circle-play">
    An active subscription with the streaming service you want to sign in to — for example Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Peacock, Max, Paramount+, or another provider. You will need your account email address and password during activation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A Supported TV or Streaming Device" icon="tv">
    A Smart TV or streaming device that has the streaming app installed and is powered on. See the **Supported Devices** section below for a full list of compatible hardware.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A Phone, Tablet, or Computer" icon="mobile">
    A second device — such as a smartphone, tablet, or laptop — with a web browser installed. You will use this device to visit the activation URL and enter your account credentials.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A Working Internet Connection" icon="wifi">
    Both your TV and your second device must be connected to the internet at the same time. The TV needs connectivity to generate and validate the activation code; your phone or computer needs it to load the activation page.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Supported Devices

TV activation is supported across a wide range of hardware, so chances are your device is compatible. **Smart TVs** from major manufacturers — including Samsung, LG, Sony, and Vizio — all support app-based activation through their built-in operating systems (Tizen, webOS, Google TV, and SmartCast respectively). **Streaming sticks and boxes** such as Roku players, Amazon Fire TV sticks and cubes, Apple TV (4th generation and later), and Google Chromecast with Google TV also fully support activation codes. Finally, **game consoles** including Sony PlayStation (PS4 and PS5) and Microsoft Xbox (One, Series S, and Series X) have streaming apps that use the same activation flow. If your device can run a streaming app, it almost certainly supports activation-code sign-in.

## Before You Begin

<Note>
  Make sure **both your TV and your phone or computer are connected to the internet** before you open the streaming app. Activation codes are time-sensitive — they typically expire after just a few minutes — so have your phone or computer within arm's reach before you launch the app on your TV. If the code expires before you finish, the TV will generate a new one and you will need to start the process over.
</Note>
